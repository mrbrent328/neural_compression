# E46-B4.1 — Factorized entity/wording generalization

## Question

Does learned activation addressing generalize because it recognizes query wording, entity representations, or both? How does retrieval change as distractors are added?

## Design

- 256 synthetic records across 8 categories (32/category).
- Randomized, category-stratified split: 24 training entities and 8 unseen entities per category. Unseen IDs are sampled across the full numeric range.
- Two familiar query templates train each seen entity; a third familiar template is used for validation/checkpoint selection.
- Two additional templates are held out as novel wording.
- Four diagnostic cells:
  1. Seen entity + familiar wording (validation; checkpoint selection).
  2. Seen entity + novel wording (held-out wording on trained entities).
  3. Unseen entity + familiar wording (training-style wording on unseen entities).
  4. Unseen entity + novel wording (both entity and wording unseen).
- Three training seeds (46, 47, 48) share one cached frozen-Qwen activation collection.
- Candidate banks are nested per query: target always present, followed by one fixed randomized distractor ordering; sizes 8, 32, 128, 256 are prefixes of that ordering.
- Margin is positive cosine score minus the highest incorrect cosine score.
- Full-bank results include per-category metrics.

## Interpretation boundaries

This remains synthetic, templated addressing research. It does not test payload reconstruction, answer quality, useful memory injection, open-domain semantics, or end-to-end compression. The validation condition is used for checkpoint selection; the other three diagnostic conditions are not. Three optimizer seeds on one randomized split provide a first variance estimate, not broad split robustness.
